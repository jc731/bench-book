# Design audit v0.5.0

## Summary

- Version 0.5.0, 2026-11-16. Built app served locally and viewed at iPhone 13 size
  (390×664 viewport), plus 360×640 for the large-roster check.
- Method: a scripted walk through every screen (setup, live, player sheet, out-player guard,
  timeout, bench, More, Log, fix sheet, quarter check, Final), with the e2e fixture's invented
  roster. Text contrast was measured on the live screen, and the player grid was measured with
  a 14-player roster.
- This is the visual and layout pass. The first-timer UX audit for this version was void
  (see `docs/ux-audit/reports/v0.5.0-void.md`), so it has no findings to pair with these.
- Each finding is labeled **measured** or **observed**, and anything not yet shown to hurt
  carries its **trigger**: what would make it worth building.

**What works:** the score, quarter and team-foul strip reads at a glance. The out-player card
and its guard sheet are clear. Every bottom-bar and sheet button is a full-thumb target. All
text on the live screen passes 7:1 contrast except the one case in D2.

**Top 3:**

1. A 14-player roster does not fit: 5 of the 15 cards, Bench included, are below the fold (D1).
2. The OUT label on an out player's card is 2.2:1 contrast: the dimming hides its own warning (D2).
3. Amber means warning, primary action, link, focus and simply "a foul happened", all at once (D3).

## Findings

### D1. A large roster pushes cards off the live screen (measured)

- With 14 players, the player grid is 750 px tall in a 474 px space on an iPhone 13 (450 px
  at 360×640). Rows 6–8, holding 4 players and the Bench / coach tech card, need a scroll.
  The grid's bottom row is cut mid-card, and an empty band sits between the grid and the
  bottom bar.
- The e2e check `expectCardsFit` only ever runs the 7-player fixture, so this has never been
  tested.
- Why it matters: the live screen is built for one-thumb, no-scroll entry. A player who
  needs a scroll costs time mid-play, and it also bears on the "only show active lineup"
  backlog item.
- **Trigger:** the owner's real roster size. If any team the app is used for carries more than
  10 players, this is a pre-1.0 fix. A compact card (shorter, or three columns) above about 10
  players is cheaper than an active lineup and keeps every player one tap away.
- Screenshot: `v0.5.0/16-live-14-players.png`

### D2. The OUT label is the dimmest thing on the out player's card (measured)

- The out card is dimmed as a whole, so its **OUT** label measures **2.2:1** (12 px bold)
  against the card. WCAG AA needs 4.5:1 at that size. The name is 3.6:1. The red border and
  dots carry the state today, but the one word that says what the state is reads the worst.
- Direction: dim the number, name and points, and keep **OUT** and the red dots at full
  strength.
- Screenshot: `v0.5.0/05-live-mid.png`

### D3. Amber does too many jobs (observed)

- On the live screen alone, amber marks:
  - **Warnings:** team fouls in the bonus, the Bonus badge, the bench tech count, a player
    at 4 fouls.
  - **The primary next action:** End Q1.
  - **Ordinary state:** a player's first foul dot. The How-to page says the dots turn amber
    at 4, but the first foul is already an amber dot.
  - **The Bench card's border.**
- **Elsewhere, amber also marks:** links (Check, Undo), the focus ring, "not saved yet" and
  the check's mismatch panel.
- When everything that matters is amber, amber stops meaning "look here". The
  four-foul warning and the bonus share a color with a button that is always on screen.
- **Trigger:** a missed bonus or 4-foul warning at a game, or a UX-audit hesitation over one.
  Direction, if it fires: keep amber for warnings only, make End Qn a neutral or white
  primary, and fill foul dots neutral until the 4th.

### D4. The Final score splits a team name across lines (observed)

- "Test Home 6 – 2 Test / Opp" wraps inside the opponent's name, so the score reads as
  "…2 Test" with "Opp" stranded below. Longer real team names make this the usual case,
  not the exception.
- Direction: one team per line with its score beside it, or the score on its own line under
  the two names.
- Screenshot: `v0.5.0/14-final.png`

### D5. The two CSVs look like a primary and an optional download (observed)

- **Book CSV** is the white primary button and **Plays CSV** is the gray secondary, but the
  coach wants both. Each one carries its own "not saved yet" line, so the visual hierarchy is
  the only thing that ranks them, and it ranks them wrongly.
- Direction: give both buttons equal weight, or add a single "Save both" primary.

### D6. On the quarter check, the bottom bars cover the play list (observed)

- The End Q1 / Undo notice plus the Later / Save bar take about a quarter of the screen.
  The first Q1 row of "Fix the plays" sits under them until you scroll.
- Direction: let the End Q1 / Undo notice go away once the check opens, since Later
  already handles leaving.
- Screenshot: `v0.5.0/13-check-mismatch.png`

### D7. The check's "Not checked" status looks like a button (observed)

- The status chip in the Check header uses the same slate fill and rounded shape as the
  app's buttons, but tapping it does nothing.
- **Trigger:** someone tapping it. Direction: show it as text, or as an outline chip with no
  fill.
